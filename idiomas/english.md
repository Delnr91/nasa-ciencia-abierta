This independent educational repository is primarily written in Spanish. This page offers **English summaries** of its five lessons; the complete lessons are currently in Spanish, and the audio is in Spanish. It is not an official NASA publication.

## 1. Why open science?

Open science is a collaborative culture that supports sharing data, information and knowledge to improve research and understanding. Transparency and collaboration can increase visibility and help others identify errors. Computational reproducibility means obtaining consistent results using the same input data, methods, code and analysis conditions.

[Full lesson in Spanish](../cursos/open-science-essentials/lecciones/01-por-que-ciencia-abierta.md)

## 2. Concerns and considerations

Researchers may worry about mistakes, being scooped, misinterpretation, credit, maintenance and sensitive data. Provide context and citation information; assess legal, privacy, institutional and community constraints before sharing. Not all research products can be released publicly.

[Full lesson in Spanish](../cursos/open-science-essentials/lecciones/02-preocupaciones-y-consideraciones.md)

## 3. Planning for open science

Plan research products throughout the project: data, software, publications, responsibilities and preservation. FAIR means **Findable, Accessible, Interoperable and Reusable**. Accessibility can include authentication or restricted access. Requirements for a management plan depend on the applicable funder and call.

[Full lesson in Spanish](../cursos/open-science-essentials/lecciones/03-planificacion-ciencia-abierta.md)

## 4. Components

Data, code, results and research papers are connected. Describe datasets with metadata, units, provenance, versions and permissions. Open-source licenses allow defined uses, modification and redistribution; open source does not always mean zero cost. A documented example helps others inspect how results were obtained.

[Full lesson and synthetic example](../cursos/open-science-essentials/lecciones/04-componentes-ciencia-abierta.md)

## 5. Tools

Preprints, repositories, version control, persistent identifiers, licenses and preregistration support different stages of research. A DOI identifies a research product; it does not certify quality. AI tools can help with research tasks but their outputs require human verification and responsible handling of inputs.

[Full lesson in Spanish](../cursos/open-science-essentials/lecciones/05-herramientas-ciencia-abierta.md)
